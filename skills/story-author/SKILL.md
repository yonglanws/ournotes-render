# Story author

`scripts/story-author.mjs` turns a JSON script into a story the story player plays. The full reference is
`docs/story-author.md` (commands, script format, error codes, fixes); this file is the working procedure.

## Before starting

- Run from the repository root with Node.js 22+. No build step and no `npm ci` are needed.
- You need a story site directory: it holds `models.json`, `stories.json` and story products
  (`nnnotes.product/1` manifests, generated by nnnotes 0.3 or later). Pass it as `--site <dir>`, or set
  `OURNOTES_SITE`. Never write into a site you cannot regenerate; work on a copy, because `build` adds files to it.
- Pictures (`render`, `base --out`, `model --out`) need Chrome, Chromium or Edge (`--chrome <path>` or `CHROME_PATH`)
  and load Live2D Cubism Core from Live2D's distribution unless `--core <file or URL>` is given. A first render takes
  about 20 seconds.
- Every command prints one JSON object on stdout; progress goes to stderr. Read `ok`, `errors` and `warnings`; exit
  status 1 means the script or job failed, 2 a usage error.

## Procedure

1. **Pick a base story.** `bases --search <text>` lists stories a script can take its scene from. Without a
   `stories.json`, name the manifest directly (`stories/<id>.json`). `base <manifest> --out shots` prints its stages,
   music cues and cast, and writes `shots/stages.png`: look at it to choose a background by id.
2. **Pick models.** `models --search <name>` lists the site's models. `model <id> --out shots` lists a model's
   motions (by stem, for example `mtn_smile01`) and expressions and draws them. Look at the pictures before choosing.
3. **Start a script.** `new <manifest> > story.json`, then edit it (format in `docs/story-author.md` and
   `docs/story-director.md`): a `cast` of model ids with display names, and `beats`; the first beat is a `scene` with
   `positions`; later beats are `say`, `narrate`, `positions`, `camera`, `fade`, `pause`, `action`, `move`, `angle`,
   `bgm`, `frame`, `frameStop`, `postEffect`, `postEffectStop`, `brightness`, `shake`, `cameraShake`, `flash`,
   `talkWindow`, `stageEnv`, `still`, `subtitles`, `hideTalk`, or another `scene` (`transition: "dissolve"` optional).
   Add `focus` and `camera` (`focusTargetHead`, `focusSharp`, `ease`) for the shot, `track` to pan, `lookAt` /
   `look` (`"stop"` to clear) for gaze, `motion`/`expression` for the speaker, and pre-line `actions` (`at: 0`) for listeners.
4. **Check.** `check story.json`. Fix errors by `path` (a JSON pointer). `--fix <file>` applies the fixes the tool
   knows (nearest valid name, renamed key, text wrapped to the talk window) and checks again; read the diff of what
   it applied before trusting it.
5. **Render and look.** `render story.json --out shots [--from n --to n]`, then open `shots/sheet.png` (every line with
   speaker and text) and the `shots/line-<n>.png` of anything doubtful. Check who is on stage, the expression, the
   camera focus, and that the text fits the talk window. `textTooWide` and `textTooTall` warnings carry fixes; breaking
   a line with `\n` is yours to place where the sentence reads well.
6. **Build.** `build story.json --name <name>` writes `custom-stories/<name>.json` into the site (a script with errors
   is not built).

## Director rules

The game's own episodes are continuous. Eight official episodes measured: two-person friendship stories run 36--44 lines in
1--2.5 minutes, cut every 3--4.5 lines, use about 1.1 motions per line, no standalone `Expression`, and a `lookAt` every
2--3 lines. 10610 (13 lines, 10 `Focus`, 5 `Track`, 20 `Motion`, no `Fade`) is an ensemble tutorial and cuts more often;
do not use its density for a daily scene.

- One stage per scene. Establish space with `scene`, then change groups with `positions`; do not open a new `scene` or
  `fade` for every beat. Open with one character (at most two), then let the next one enter after 2--4 lines. At most two
  `positions` changes; keep each character's slot stable (moving someone from `left` to `center` compiles to `Out` + `In`
  and flashes).
- Cut in groups of 3--4 lines: two-shot (`middle`, `focus: 5`) and a speaker close-up (`close`, `focus` that cast)
  alternate. Change `focus` only when the active group changes or at an emotional turn; do not cut every line, and do
  not stay in one static shot for the whole scene. A 30-line scene needs roughly 7--10 `Focus` changes. `full` (1.0x)
  and `near` (1.2x) are wide establishing shots, at most once per scene; `close` (2.8x) is the close-up, about one per
  8 lines (2--5 in 30 lines) and must recover within 1--3 lines to `middle`/`far` (unless ending the scene), never hold
  the same `close` indefinitely. `middle` (1.4x) is the default two-shot, `far` (1.8x) for a 3-person group,
  `very-close` (4x) and `macro` (6x) extreme close-ups (at most once). A slow `zoom` (1.03--1.10 in, 0.92--0.98 out,
  `zoomDuration` 3--5, `ease: "OutSine"`) is optional, one per shot at most.
- `focus` may be a cast id, a position, or a focus point 1--9: 1/3/5/7/9 are the slots, 2/4/6/8 frame a pair. Prefer a
  focus point or position; a character `focus` is for close-ups and emotional turns (`focusTargetHead: true` locks camera
  height to the target head). Pair `focus` with `track` (roughly -0.8..0.8 for two people, clamped to +/-0.25 in `close`
  and 0 in `very-close`/`macro`); 0--2 `track` rows per scene, usually 0--1 for two people.
  Two people on stage stand at `left` / `right` (slots 3/7) with `focus: 5`; never use `center+right` or `center+left`
  (those overlap), and avoid overly wide `far-left`/`far-right` unless blocking a 3-person group. Three people use
  `far-left` / `center` / `far-right` with `camera: far`, `focus: 5`, `track: 0` (use position objects `{ at, dz: 0.6..1.5 }`
  for subtle Z-depth staggering); `story.maxOnStage` defaults to 3, and 2 keeps only the two-person `left`/`right` pair.
- Use motivated cinematography and FX rather than arbitrary PanV2/Pedestal wobbles on static 2-person dialogue:
  `OutSine` `zoom` + background `dof` (`dofLayers: 0`; never blur the focused speaker; character `dofLayers: 2` must name
  a non-speaker `dofTarget`) + optional `bgm: "stop"` on emotional turns; multi-line `PanV2` drifts; `hideTalk: true` +
  `pause >= 0.6` for silent beats; `thought: true` / `ignoreLipSync: true` for inner monologue and ellipsis lines (compiler
  auto-sets `IgnoreLipSync: 1` on voiceless ellipsis); `move` (`to`, `dir`, `dx/dy/dz`) for shifts/crouching; `angle`
  tilts; `shake` (`target: "ui"`) for dialog outbursts; `brightness`/`frame` (`frameAction: "Show_anime"`, `frameStop`)/
  `postEffect` (`postEffectStop`)/`fadeRule` (`0001`..`0005`)/`transition: "dissolve"` for flashbacks; downcast `look`
  (`lookY: -0.5..-0.8`) paired with `look: "stop"`. Always close open effects/gaze before a scene change or episode end.
- One main action for the speaker (`say.motion`) and at most one listener reaction in `actions` at `0`. Official episodes
  have no mid-line actions, so write `at: 0`; roughly 6% of lines have no motion at all. The compiler fades
  each motion in from the current pose (the clip's own 1 s fade), so you do not need extra beats to cover a switch.
  Name motions by stem (`mtn_smile01`); the compiler chooses `_R` on left slots (1, 3), `_C` at center (5), and `_L` on
  right slots (7, 9) toward conversation partners -- specify a full `_L`/`_C`/`_R` name only when deliberately turning away.
- About 20--26 motions per 20 lines: roughly 12--16 lines with one, 3--5 with two, 3--4 with none. Prefer
  `nod` / `smile` / `check` / `question` / `thinking` / `denial` / `serious`; `surprised` 1--2 times, `angry` and `kime`
  at most once. Repeating a gentle gesture is natural.
- Attach `expression` to a motion. Keep the default `idle`; do not smile on every line, and use a standalone
  `Expression` (no motion) at most once per scene.
- Two-person scenes need a `lookAt` every 2--3 lines; with three or more, use it only when addressed or reacting.
- Vary line length: 20--35% of lines take two rows, at least one 2--6 character reaction every 5--6 lines, and each
  character keeps a distinct rhythm (quiet: short and ellipsis-heavy; outgoing: longer, two rows, exclamations).
- Use `pause` sparingly (0.1--0.5 s) and `fade out`/`fade in` only for a real time or place jump. `action` beats are
  silent reaction shots.
- Every name must exist in the base site; `focus`/`lookAt`/`actions` targets must be on stage.

## Script rules that are easy to miss

- A motion or expression needs the speaker on stage; a speaker off stage only warns (`speakerOffStage`).
- Motions are named by stem; the variant facing the character's side plays. Use a full clip name only to force one.
- Two characters cannot share a position (`far-left`, `left`, `center`, `right`, `far-right`).
- `focus` 2/4/6/8 are composition points between slots, not places to stand; they frame two characters.
- `track` is a camera offset in stage units (-4..4), not a position; it compiles to the game's `Track` row.
- Text is literal: `<` and `\` are characters, and `\n` in the JSON string breaks the line.
- `language` must be one of the base story's languages; fonts are `sans`, `serif`, `rounded` or a CSS family list.
- Re-render only what changed with `--from` and `--to`; each render replaces the pictures in `--out`.

## Common failures

| Message | Cause and action |
|---|---|
| `a base story needs a story product … earlier format` | The site predates nnnotes 0.3. Generate it again with nnnotes 0.3 or later. |
| `bases` returns `count: 0` with a `note` | The site has no `stories.json`; use `base`/`new` with a manifest path. |
| Rendering fails to start a browser | Install Chrome, Chromium or Edge, or pass `--chrome`. |
| Rendering hangs on Live2D | Cubism Core could not be fetched; pass `--core <file or URL>`. |
